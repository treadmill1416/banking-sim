Net Interest Margin

Net Interest Margin (NIM) measures the difference between interest income earned on loans and reserves versus interest paid on deposits and CB borrowing, relative to total earning assets. It is annualized and expressed as a percentage.

A positive NIM means your bank is earning more on its assets than it costs to fund them. A narrowing NIM signals compressed margins — consider adjusting loan or deposit rates.

Tutorial

What Is Bank Runner?

You run a commercial bank. Your goal is to grow deposits, issue profitable loans, manage risks, and survive — through economic booms, recessions, bank runs, and central bank policy changes.

Your Bank

The Dashboard shows your balance sheet: Cash (reserves at the central bank), Loans (outstanding principal), Deposits (customer funding), and Equity (net worth). If equity goes negative, you're insolvent — game over.

Setting Rates

On the Policies tab, set your Loan Rate (what borrowers pay) and Deposit Rate (what you pay savers). A higher loan rate means more income per loan but fewer borrowers. A higher deposit rate attracts more deposits but costs you more. Find the sweet spot.

Approving Loans

The Loans tab lists pending applications. Each shows a default probability. Use the slider to adjust the rate, pick a term (12/24/36 months), then click Approve or Reject. Approved loans immediately create a deposit in the borrower's account — this is how banks create money.

Managing Risk

Some loans will default, eating into your equity. The Pricing Curve on the Dashboard helps set rate based on risk. Keep an eye on your Reserve Ratio (cash / deposits) — if it falls below 1%, the central bank will step in. Negative equity accelerates deposit outflows and can trigger a bank run.

External Forces

The ECB adjusts three policy rates (Deposit Facility, Main Refi Rate, Lending Facility). The economy cycles through Boom → Normal → Recession regimes, affecting loan demand, default rates, and deposit flows. Watch the regime badge in the header.

Growing Your Bank

Hire Loan Officers (Policies tab) to handle more loans. Hire Risk Analysts (Research tab) to earn research points, then spend those points on Default Risk Estimation (more accurate risk data), Automated Underwriting (auto-approve/reject), Marketing (boost loan demand and deposits), and Branch Expansion (more loan capacity and demand).

How Banks Create Money

When a bank approves a loan, it does not hand over existing cash. Instead, it credits the borrower's deposit account with a new deposit — new money that did not exist before. This is credit creation: loans create deposits, not the other way around. The reserve requirement (1% of deposits) is a post-facto constraint, not a funding limit. In this simulation, every approved loan increases the deposit balance on your balance sheet immediately, illustrating how the banking system creates the majority of the broad money supply.
